## 1. CSLB contractor licensing: classifications, minor-work exemption, advertising, bonds/WC, verification, penalties, platform rules

### Takeaway
Plumbing (C-36) and electrical (C-10) pros, and B general contractors, must hold a CSLB license and show the license number in every ad, online listings included. The handyman "minor work" exemption rose from $500 to $1,000 on Jan 1, 2025 (AB 2622). It does NOT cover permit-required or behind-the-wall plumbing/electrical work, or anyone who has helpers. So in practice nearly every plumber or electrician listed on NextTrust must be licensed, and the platform should verify licenses against CSLB data, which is available as a free bulk download (no official real-time API was found).

### Cited Findings
**Minor-work ("handyman") exemption: changed to $1,000**
- AB 2622 (Carrillo, Ch. 240, Stats. 2024) raised the minor-work exemption from $500 to $1,000, effective **January 1, 2025**. It amended B&P Code §§ 7027.2 and 7048. — [CSLB AB 2622 implementation bulletin](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/IndustryBulletins/2024/AB%202622%20Implementation.FINAL.pdf); [USD CPIL blog](https://sites.sandiego.edu/cpil-blog/2024/10/09/governor-signs-bill-increasing-minor-work-exemption-for-unlicensed-contracting-from-500-to-1000/); [CSLB press release](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/PressReleases/2024/AB2622.FINAL.pdf)
- Conditions: the work must not require a building permit, and the unlicensed person may not employ anyone on the project. If a permit is required, or the person hires one or more helpers, a license is required even under $1,000. — [CSLB bulletin (via search summary)](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/IndustryBulletins/2024/AB%202622%20Implementation.FINAL.pdf); [South Tahoe Now](https://southtahoenow.com/story/12/30/2024/new-california-law-2025unlicensed-handyperson-jobs-increase-500-1000)
- Per legislative summaries, the exemption does not cover behind-the-wall mechanical, electrical, and plumbing work, structural work, or other specified public-safety trades. — [USD CPIL blog](https://sites.sandiego.edu/cpil-blog/2024/10/09/governor-signs-bill-increasing-minor-work-exemption-for-unlicensed-contracting-from-500-to-1000/)
- The exemption is unavailable to anyone who advertises that they are licensed or qualified to act as a contractor. CSLB said it would monitor complaints in the $500–$1,000 range for consumer harm. — [CSLB bulletin (via search summary)](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/IndustryBulletins/2024/AB%202622%20Implementation.FINAL.pdf)
- Some consumer guides still quote the outdated $500 figure. — (observed across search results; e.g., [housecallpro](https://www.housecallpro.com/licensing/handyman/california/))

**Classifications**
- C-10 = electrical contractor. A "General Electrician" (DIR certification) is defined as one who works for a C-10 contractor on any NEC-covered system. — [8 CCR § 290.1 (DIR)](https://dir.ca.gov/t8/290_1.html)
- C-36 (plumbing) and B (general building) are standard CSLB classifications. — [open-exam-prep CSLB classifications (secondary)](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/ca-general-contractor/cslb-law-and-governance/cslb-structure-and-classifications) (verify scope rules on cslb.ca.gov)

**Advertising: license number and scope**
- B&P § 7027.1: it is a misdemeanor for any person to advertise construction work covered by the Contractors' License Law unless they hold a valid license **in the classification advertised**. Advertising outside one's licensed scope is also covered. — [§ 7027.1 (public.law)](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_7027.1); [open-exam-prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/ca-hvac-contractor/cslb-licensing-law/advertising-disclosure-subcontracting)
- B&P § 7030.5: licensees must include their license number in all advertising, including online postings and websites, print, radio, TV, and even vehicle decals. — [CSLB vehicle advertising bulletin 20-21](https://cslb.ca.gov/Resources/IndustryBulletins/2020/20-21_Advertising_on_Vehicles.pdf); [CSLB newsletter 2019](https://www2.cslb.ca.gov/Newsletter/2019-Spring/3-Advertising-Rules.asp); [§ 7030.5 (FindLaw)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-7030-5/)
- CSLB consumer guidance: "all contractor advertisements, including online ads, must have the contractor's state license number", but a displayed number does not by itself mean the license is valid. — [CSLB: finding the right contractor](https://cslb.ca.gov/consumers/hire_a_contractor/finding_the_right_contractor.aspx)
- Civil penalties for licensees who violate advertising laws: $100–$5,000 (16 CCR § 884). CSLB can cite unlicensed persons under § 7027.1 for directory advertising. — [CSLB newsletter (Summer 2016)](https://www2.cslb.ca.gov/Newsletter/2016-summer/advertising.asp); [CSLB 2014 ad guidelines](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Newsletter/2014-Fall/ad%20guidelines.asp)
- No dollar threshold was found for the license-number requirement. It applies to all licensee advertising. (Search did not surface any "$500" cutoff for § 7030.5.)

**Online marketplaces specifically**
- CSLB has published an "Online Marketplace Fast Facts" sheet and a 2018 industry bulletin, "Clearing Up Confusion about License and Contracting Requirements for Online Home Improvement Marketplace Companies". The core point: whatever the platform type, **the contractor performing the work must hold a CSLB license**. The open question CSLB addresses is when the marketplace itself must be licensed. — [CSLB Online Marketplace Fast Facts](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/IndustryBulletins/Online_Marketplace_Fast_Facts.pdf); [CSLB 2018 bulletin](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Media_Room/Industry_Bulletins/2018/July_20.aspx) (full text not retrievable from this session; see Gaps)

**Bond and workers' compensation**
- SB 216 (2022) phased in mandatory workers' comp for C-8, C-20, C-22, and D-49 from Jan 1, 2023, and originally for **all** active licensees, with or without employees, from Jan 1, 2026. — [CSLB WC bulletin](https://cslb.ca.gov/Resources/IndustryBulletins/2022/Industry_Bulletin_workers_comp.pdf); [CSLB 2023 new-laws bulletin](https://cslb.ca.gov/Resources/IndustryBulletins/2022/Industry_Bulletin-2023_New_Laws.pdf)
- **CHANGED:** SB 1455 (2024, CSLB sunset bill) delayed the all-licensee requirement to **January 1, 2028**. Until then, licensees in most trades (all except C-39, C-8, C-20, C-22, D-49) may keep claiming the "no employees" exemption. CSLB must also build a process to verify exemption eligibility. So **C-10 and C-36 solo licensees can currently hold a WC exemption, but only if they truly have no employees**. — [CSLB SB 1455 changes bulletin](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/IndustryBulletins/2024/SB%201455%20changes.FINAL.pdf); [Business Insurance](https://www.businessinsurance.com/california-governor-signs-bill-to-delay-comp-mandate-for-contractors/); [servicemag](https://servicemag.org/news/california-sb216-workers-comp-contractors)
- SB 291 is described as tightening enforcement against abuse of the WC exemption. — [sitevetter (secondary)](https://www.sitevetter.com/blog/sb-291-workers-comp-enforcement-2026/) (unverified detail)
- Contractor license bond: most sources give **$25,000** (raised by SB 607). One guide says $12,500 for C-10, which appears outdated. — [housecallpro](https://www.housecallpro.com/licensing/general-contractor/california/); [thecontractormatrix 2026](https://www.thecontractormatrix.com/contractor-license-requirements/california); conflicting: [1800insurance](https://www.1800insurance.com/guides/california-electrical-contractor-insurance-requirements)

**License verification: lookup, data download, API**
- CSLB public license lookup ("Check a License") is free online, or by phone at 800-321-CSLB (2752). — [CSLB check license](https://cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/checklicenseii/CheckLicense.aspx); [CSLB consumer page](https://cslb.ca.gov/consumers/hire_a_contractor/finding_the_right_contractor.aspx)
- **CSLB Data Portal: free downloadable "Master List of California Licensed Contractors"**, split into 3 files: License Master, Workers' Compensation, Personnel. It includes current licenses and expired-but-renewable licenses, and excludes cancelled, revoked, and expired non-renewable ones. Each file is dated. There is also a "List by Classification and County" download, which is useful for a San Mateo/Santa Clara C-10/C-36 seed list. — [CSLB Data Portal – Contractor List](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/DataPortal/ContractorList); [CSLB Data Portal](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/onlineservices/dataportal/); [List by Classification & County](https://cslb.ca.gov/Onlineservices/DataPortal/ListByCounty)
- Paid bulk "Full File / Update File" orders are also available (fixed-width layout LBL780J1, 700,000+ records). An older form lists $235 per file; confirm current pricing with the CSLB Data Services Unit (916-255-3975). — [Full/Update file order form](https://WWW.CSLB.CA.GOV/Resources/FormsAndApplications/FULL_FILE_-_UPDATE_FILE_ORDER_FORM.pdf); [Record layout](https://WWW.CSLB.CA.GOV/Resources/FormsAndApplications/License_Master_Record_Layout_LBL780J1.pdf)
- No official CSLB REST API was found. Third parties (Apify scrapers, Prelien) mirror the public data, with Prelien describing roughly monthly refreshes. — [Apify CSLB scraper](https://apify.com/scrapers_lat/california-cslb-scraper.md); [Prelien developers](https://www.prelien.co/developers)

### Inferences
- **Product rule:** treat every plumbing or electrical listing as requiring a CSLB license (C-36 for plumbing, C-10 for electrical, or B where the scope fits). The $1,000 exemption is too narrow to rely on: permit-required work, behind-the-wall MEP work, and anyone with a helper are all excluded. If a "handyman" tier is ever added, restrict it to sub-$1,000, non-permit, non-MEP tasks with no helpers, and do not label those people "licensed" or "qualified contractors".
- **Show the license number on every pro profile, search card, and the "online now" map pin/card.** Each of these is arguably an "advertisement" under § 7030.5. Enforce scope matching: a C-36 company should not be shown for electrical jobs (§ 7027.1 bars advertising outside the classification).
- **Verification pipeline:** at onboarding, match the license number, business name, and qualifier/personnel against the CSLB Master List. Re-check at least each time a new CSLB file is published, and auto-hide pros whose license is suspended or expired, whose bond has lapsed, or whose WC policy has lapsed. Also store the WC policy or exemption status from the WC file. A pro with an "exempt" WC status who registers technicians on NextTrust is a red flag: having employees is inconsistent with the exemption.
- For companies registering technicians: the license belongs to the company (via its qualifier). Technicians work under that license, so the profile should show the company's license number, not imply the technician is personally licensed.

## 2. Electrician certification (DIR) and plumbing/electrical permits

### Takeaway
Electricians employed by a C-10 contractor must hold a DIR state certification (General, Residential, etc.) or be registered as electrician trainees (Labor Code § 3099 et seq.). The C-10 qualifying person, and per secondary sources a sole owner doing their own work, are exempt. There is no state certification for plumbers. In Bay Area cities, nearly all water heater replacements and panel upgrades need a city permit.

### Cited Findings
- DIR Electrician Certification Program: "General Electrician" = works for a C-10 on any NEC-covered system. A separate "Residential Electrician" track covers single-family and multi-family dwellings. Older regulation text lists 8,000 hours of experience for General vs 4,800 for Residential; verify against current text. — [8 CCR § 290.1](https://dir.ca.gov/t8/290_1.html); [8 CCR § 291.1](https://dir.ca.gov/t8/291_1.html); [DIR ECU](https://dir.ca.gov/dlse/ECU/ECUonline.html)
- The qualifying person for a C-10 license need not also be certified under § 3099 to do electrical work for that contractor. — [Labor Code § 3099.2 (2011 version via Justia, per search summary)](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2011/lab/division-3/3070-3099.5/3099.2)
- Sole owners holding the C-10 in their own name reportedly need no DIR certification to do the work themselves (secondary source). — [permitflow (secondary)](https://www.permitflow.com/blog/california-electrical-license)
- Uncertified workers may work for a C-10 contractor only if registered as an **electrician trainee** (and enrolled in an approved program). — [DIR: What is an electrician trainee?](https://dir.ca.gov/DLSE/ECU/ElectricalTrainee.htm)
- **Change flagged:** the Labor Commissioner is implementing new exam scheduling procedures for certification exams taken on or after June 1, 2026. — [DIR ECU](https://dir.ca.gov/dlse/ECU/ECUonline.html)
- Permits (Bay Area examples; no Redwood City page was retrieved): San Mateo County says California requires permits for nearly all water heater work. — [SMC water heater permit](https://www.smcgov.org/planning/water-heater-permit)
  - San José: water heater installation or replacement requires a plumbing permit, possibly also an electrical permit. Electrical panel upgrades require permits. — [San José water heaters](https://www.sanjoseca.gov/businesses/development-services-permit-center/start-your-project/water-heaters); [San José appliance installations](https://www.sanjoseca.gov/businesses/development-services-permit-center/start-your-project/appliance-installations)
  - Menlo Park (neighbor of Redwood City): load calculations are required for heat-pump water heater electrification when the panel is under 200 A, and a fuel-switch water heater replacement needs a building permit. — [Menlo Park electrification permit reqs](https://menlopark.gov/Government/Departments/City-Managers-Office/Sustainability/Home-Electrification/Existing-building-electrification-permit-requirements); [Menlo Park water heater submittal](https://www.menlopark.gov/Government/Departments/Community-Development/Building/Repairs-to-Existing-Structures-Permit-Information/Water-Heater-Replacement-Submittal-Requirements)
  - Mountain View, Milpitas, and Morgan Hill also publish water heater permit pages. — [Mountain View](https://developmentpermits.mountainview.gov/residential/water-heater-and-bracing); [Milpitas](https://www.milpitas.gov/365/Water-Heater-Installation); [Morgan Hill](https://morganhill.ca.gov/168/Water-Heater-Replacement)

### Inferences
- For company accounts that register technicians: collect each electrician technician's DIR certification number (or trainee registration) and expiry, and show "State-certified electrician" only when verified. Solo C-10 owners working alone don't need it.
- Plumbing technicians have no state certification. Verification relies on the company's C-36 license.
- Add a "permit likely required" flag to job types (water heater swap, panel upgrade, EV charger, repiping, gas line, HPWH conversion). Price recommendations should note that permit fees are extra. Because permit-required work falls outside the $1,000 exemption, these job types must route only to licensed pros.
- Jurisdiction varies city by city (Redwood City, San Mateo, Menlo Park, San José, unincorporated San Mateo County). Consider a per-city permit link table.

### Gaps
- Redwood City Building Division rules were not retrieved. Check Redwood City's permit portal for over-the-counter/online permits for water heaters and panels.
- Current text of Labor Code §§ 3099–3099.5, including the exact exemptions (e.g., certain low-voltage or residential work), was not verified on leginfo.

## 3. Worker classification (AB5) and marketplace liability for damage

### Takeaway
AB5's ABC test (Labor Code § 2775) is the default. The "referral agency" exemption (§ 2777) can let a marketplace's relationship with service providers be judged under the looser Borello test, but it has strict conditions and construction-type exclusions. A lawyer must confirm whether plumbing and electrical services fit. A marketplace that only lists licensed businesses, does not set prices, and does not control the work is in a stronger position. Companies listing their own technicians are responsible for classifying those technicians (generally W-2 employees, with WC).

### Cited Findings
- § 2777 (added by AB 2257, eff. 9/4/2020) governs the relationship between a "referral agency" and its "service providers". Whether an individual worker is an employee of the service provider or of the client remains governed by the ABC test (§ 2775). — [Labor Code § 2777 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-lab/division-3/chapter-2/article-1-5/section-2777); [california.public.law § 2777](https://california.public.law/codes/labor_code_section_2777)
- A referral agency is a business that provides clients with referrals for service providers to provide services under a contract. If all conditions are met, Borello governs whether the provider is an IC or an employee of the agency. — [tbowleslaw](https://www.tbowleslaw.com/blogs/californias-newest-independent-contractor-law-part-2); [natlawreview on AB 2257](https://natlawreview.com/article/ab-2257-new-exemptions-added-and-modifications-made-to-california-s-landmark-worker)
- The listed services (non-exclusive) include graphic design, web design, photography, tutoring, consulting, youth sports coaching, caddying, event planning, etc. Commentary indicates the exclusions cover **construction services other than minor home repair**, as well as high-hazard occupations. Plumbing and electrical were not confirmed by name in the retrieved snippets. — [natlawreview](https://natlawreview.com/article/ab-2257-new-exemptions-added-and-modifications-made-to-california-s-landmark-worker); [Littler AB 2257 comparison](https://littler.com/sites/default/files/ab_2257_compared_to_ab_5_legislative_format_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Structure to reduce AB5 risk:** (a) prefer listing **business entities** (licensed companies and sole proprietors operating under their own CSLB license) that have their own business licenses, insurance, and customers. (b) Do not require pros to accept jobs, wear branding, or follow platform work methods. (c) Let pros set their own prices: "platform-recommended prices" should be advisory only, never mandatory or penalized. (d) Charge a flat subscription rather than a cut of each job. (e) Do not pay pros or collect payment on their behalf (true at launch).
- Licensed solo C-10/C-36 contractors are themselves "contractors" under CSLB law. Background knowledge (unverified, Labor Code § 2750.5) suggests an unlicensed person doing licensed work is presumed an employee of whoever hired them. That is another reason to list only licensed pros.
- **Companies listing technicians:** the company remains responsible for each technician's classification, WC, and wage-and-hour compliance. NextTrust's ToS should make that explicit, require the company to warrant that technicians are its employees (or properly classified) and covered by WC, and should not create any direct contract between NextTrust and technicians.
- **Damage liability:** platform liability for a pro's negligence depends on control and representations. Marketing language like "vetted", "guaranteed", or "NextTrust Certified" can create negligent-selection or misrepresentation exposure. Mitigations: ToS stating the platform is a venue and not a party to the service contract, liability caps, a requirement that pros carry general liability insurance (collect certificates), an accurate description of what verification covers, an optional limited "guarantee" priced with insurance, and the platform's own general and professional (tech E&O) liability insurance. No source was retrieved on CA case law for home-services marketplaces; **lawyer needed**.

### Gaps
- Exact text of § 2777's exclusions, and whether plumbing or electrical counts as excluded construction, could not be confirmed (Justia blocked). Verify on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov.
- No primary sources were retrieved on marketplace negligence or vicarious liability for home-services platforms in California.

## 4. Reviews: FTC rule (2024) and California law

### Takeaway
FTC 16 CFR Part 465 (effective Oct 21, 2024) bans fake reviews, incentives conditioned on review sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews, review suppression, and fake social-influence indicators, with civil penalties for first-time knowing violations. California Civil Code § 1670.8 bans contract terms that restrict consumers' negative reviews. A "verified reviews" system must tie each review to a real booking and show negative reviews too.

### Cited Findings
- FTC Final Rule (16 CFR Part 465), effective **October 21, 2024**, prohibits: writing, selling, or buying fake or false consumer reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones and reviews by people with no experience of the service; offering compensation or incentives **conditioned on** positive or negative sentiment; insider or employee reviews without clear disclosure of the material connection; company-controlled "independent" review sites; review suppression (unfounded legal threats, intimidation, or misrepresenting that the displayed reviews are all or most of the reviews when negatives were suppressed); and buying or selling fake social-media influence indicators. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [WSGR](https://wsgr.com/en/insights/ftc-issues-final-rule-banning-fake-and-misleading-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials.html); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/ftc-publishes-final-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials)
- Civil penalties of roughly $51,744 per violation (2024 figure, inflation-adjusted yearly) are available for violations with actual or "fairly implied" knowledge. This lets the FTC seek penalties for **first** violations. For procuring or disseminating fake reviews, the standard is that the business "knew or should have known". — [DWT](https://dwt.com/insights/2024/08/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews); [Manatt](https://manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/ftc-issues-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-and-tes)
- California Civil Code § 1670.8 ("Yelp law") voids consumer-contract clauses waiving the right to make statements about the business. Penalties: up to $2,500 for a first violation, $5,000 for each later one, and up to $10,000 if willful, intentional, or reckless. Businesses may still remove reviews that are otherwise lawful to remove. — [Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/california-bans-non-disparagement-clauses-in-consumer-contracts.html); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2024/02/Website-Terms-of-Use-May-Risk-Liability-Under-California-Law)
- Nearly 20 putative class actions since Nov 2023 have targeted website terms containing non-disparagement language. — [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2024/02/Website-Terms-of-Use-May-Risk-Liability-Under-California-Law)
- A 2026 decision (Arterberry v. Peet's) reportedly limits private penalty suits to cases where the seller tries to enforce the clause. Court and date were not confirmed. — [Eric Goldman blog, July 2026](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2026/07/another-court-shuts-the-door-on-yelp-law-drive-by-lawsuits-arterberry-v-peets.htm)

### Inferences (review-system design)
- **Must:** only allow reviews from clients with a completed booking, and label them "Verified booking". Publish negative reviews under the same rules as positive ones. Have a written, neutral moderation policy that removes reviews only for objective reasons (spam, hate, privacy, off-topic, conflict of interest) and applies it equally. Disclose how the star average is computed and any reviews not shown. Detect and block reviews from the pro's own employees or relatives, and self-reviews.
- **Must not:** let pros pay to remove or hide reviews, or let subscription tier affect which reviews are shown. Do not offer pros or clients incentives tied to positive reviews; a neutral incentive for any honest review is allowed but must be disclosed. Do not seed the platform with fabricated or AI-generated reviews, or import reviews without disclosing the source. Do not threaten reviewers or include non-disparagement terms in client or pro ToS (§ 1670.8).
- Sorting by "recommended" when paid placement influences ranking needs clear "Sponsored" labeling (FTC deception principles; general inference).
- Background knowledge (unverified): 47 U.S.C. § 230 generally shields the platform from liability for third-party review content. It does not shield the platform's own fake or edited reviews.

### Gaps
- Current (2026) inflation-adjusted FTC penalty amount not retrieved.
- Interaction with the federal Consumer Review Fairness Act (15 U.S.C. § 45b) was not researched.

## 5. CCPA/CPRA (incl. precise location for "online now" map) and background checks (ICRAA, Fair Chance Act)

### Takeaway
The CCPA applies once NextTrust exceeds $26.625M in annual revenue (2025–26 figure), handles personal information of 100,000+ consumers/households, or earns 50%+ of revenue from selling or sharing personal information. A startup may be below the thresholds at launch, but should design for compliance now. Pros' live GPS position is **sensitive personal information** (precise geolocation = within a 1,850-ft radius), which triggers "Limit the Use" obligations. Platform background checks on pros implicate ICRAA's 7-year conviction lookback and its consent and disclosure rules.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 CPI-adjusted revenue threshold for a CCPA "business" (Civ. Code § 1798.140(d)(1)(A)): **$26,625,000**, up from $25M. Adjusted in odd-numbered years, with the next adjustment Jan 1, 2027. The revenue test is worldwide gross revenue for the prior calendar year. — [CPPA CPI adjustment page](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/regulations/cpi_adjustment.html); [CPPA announcement Dec 2024](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20241217.html); [Jackson Lewis FAQ](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/navigating-california-consumer-privacy-act-30-essential-faqs-covered-businesses-including-clarifying-regulations-effective-1126)
- CCPA fine amounts were also CPI-adjusted for 2025. — [CPPA announcement](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20241217.html); [FMG Law](https://www.fmglaw.com/cyber-privacy-security/key-updates-to-ccpa-fines-and-penalties-for-2025/)
- "Precise geolocation" = data derived from a device and used to locate a consumer within a circle with a radius of **1,850 feet** (Civ. Code § 1798.140(ae)). It is sensitive personal information. — [CPPA AB 1355 memo](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/meetings/materials/20250501_item3_ab1355.pdf); [BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/print/v2/content/1518956/precise-geolocation-recent-trends-and-enforcement.pdf); one secondary source says 1,750 ft, which is the figure in other states' laws.
- Right to limit use of sensitive PI (§ 1798.121): businesses using SPI beyond permitted purposes must offer a "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information" link. After a request, use is restricted to purposes such as providing the services a consumer would reasonably expect. This applies even if the business never sells or shares data. — [Clym](https://www.clym.io/blog/handling-requests-to-limit-the-use-of-sensitive-personal-information-under-the-ccpa); [BCLP SPI guide](https://www.bclplaw.com/print/v2/content/1516870/sensitive-personal-information-understanding-and-complying-with-the-new-rules-in-the-united-states.pdf)
- Pending or new location legislation: AB 1355 (2025, the "California Location Privacy Act") and AB 322 were tracked by the CPPA. They would add location-specific rules using the same 1,850-ft standard. Final status not verified. — [CPPA AB 1355 memo](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/meetings/materials/20250501_item3_ab1355.pdf); [CPPA AB 322 memo](https://cppa.ca.gov/meetings/materials/20250724_item4_ab322_memo.pdf)
- ICRAA (Civ. Code § 1786 et seq.): investigative consumer reports may not include convictions more than 7 years old (§ 1786.18(a)(7)) or non-conviction arrests. Unlike the FCRA, there is no salary threshold. Liability is reportedly $10,000 per violation or actual damages, plus attorney's fees (secondary source). — [open-exam-prep ICRAA (secondary)](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/ca-private-investigator/california-privacy-statutory-restrictions/background-checks-icraa-compliance); [Nakase Law ICRAA](https://nakaselawfirm.com/?p=21904); [Seyfarth Cal-Peculiarities](https://content.seyfarth.com/publications/Cal-Peculiarities-2024/78)
- Gig platforms such as TaskRabbit publish California background-check notices citing Civ. Code § 1786.22 (the subject's right to inspect their file), which suggests marketplaces treat ICRAA as applicable to provider screening. — [TaskRabbit CA notice](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002056203-Notice-Regarding-Background-Checks-Per-California-Law)

### Inferences
- **"Online now" map design (privacy by design):** (1) Show pros' location **coarsened**: snap to a grid or show neighborhood/ZIP centroid or a radius larger than 1,850 ft, so the public map does not publish precise geolocation of individuals. (2) Collect precise GPS only with explicit opt-in, only while "online", and retain it briefly (e.g., minutes or hours, not history). (3) If a solo pro's position is shown, that is the precise location of a natural person, which carries stalking/safety risk; a coarse display also reduces that risk. (4) Client location (service address) is PI. Clients' device geolocation for "near me" should be optional, with ZIP entry as the alternative.
- Notice at collection, a privacy policy listing categories, purposes, and retention periods, DSAR handling, and Global Privacy Control honoring are needed if covered. If ad pixels "share" data for cross-context behavioral advertising, a "Do Not Sell or Share" link is needed. Being under the thresholds at launch reduces legal exposure, but marketplaces commonly comply anyway, and the 100,000-consumer prong can be reached quickly across clients plus pros.
- Pros are CCPA "consumers" too. Data about sole proprietors is PI, and B2B/HR exemptions expired Jan 1, 2023 (background knowledge, unverified here).
- **Background checks:** if NextTrust screens pros (e.g., via Checkr), use a CA-compliant vendor flow: a standalone disclosure, written authorization, a checkbox to receive a copy, a 7-year limit, and pre-adverse and adverse action notices. Pros are not employees, so whether the FCRA/ICRAA "employment purpose" rules and the Fair Chance Act (Gov. Code § 12952, which applies to employers with 5+ employees) technically apply is uncertain. Following them anyway is the safer course. The SF Fair Chance Ordinance may also be relevant for SF operations. **Employment/consumer-reporting lawyer needed.**
- Alternative to criminal background checks at launch: rely on CSLB licensing (CSLB itself fingerprints license applicants, per background knowledge, unverified), plus identity verification, insurance certificates, and reviews.

### Gaps
- California Fair Chance Act and SF Fair Chance Ordinance applicability to independent-contractor platforms: no sources retrieved.
- Whether the 100,000-consumer threshold is CPI-adjusted (believed not) was not confirmed.
- New CPPA regulations (risk assessments, cybersecurity audits, ADMT), finalized 2025 with phased effective dates from 2026, were not researched here. They may apply to geolocation processing and "recommended price" algorithms. Verify on cppa.ca.gov.

## 6. Recommended/dynamic pricing and price gouging (Penal Code § 396)

### Takeaway
After a declared state of emergency (federal, state, or local), Penal Code § 396 caps price increases at 10% over pre-emergency prices. For repair and reconstruction services, the cap lasts **180 days** (vs 30 days for most goods), and violations are misdemeanors. A platform that recommends or auto-raises prices ("surge") during emergencies could facilitate violations, so recommended prices need an emergency-mode cap.

### Cited Findings
- During a declared emergency, it is unlawful to sell or offer covered goods or services at more than **10% above** the price charged immediately before the declaration. For contractors, the alternative baseline is no more than 10% over cost plus the contractor's customarily applied markup. — [Penal Code § 396 (public.law)](https://california.public.law/codes/penal_code_section_396); [Cal OES price gouging](https://caloes.ca.gov/cal-oes-divisions/legal-affairs/price-gouging)
- Protections for **repair or reconstruction services** and emergency clean-up last **180 days** after the declaration. Most other goods and services are covered for 30 days, and officials can extend both periods. — [Santa Monica](https://santamonica.gov/blog/california-s-anti-price-gouging-law-prohibits-contractors-from-over-charging-customers-for-repair-and-emergency-clean-up-services-during-declared-emergency); [Cal OES](https://caloes.ca.gov/cal-oes-divisions/legal-affairs/price-gouging); [Shouse](https://www.shouselaw.com/ca/defense/penal-code/396/)
- Penalty: misdemeanor, up to 1 year in jail and/or up to $10,000 fine. Also civil enforcement under the UCL. — [egattorneys](https://www.egattorneys.com/price-gouging-penal-code-396); [Reed Smith on 2025 LA wildfire enforcement](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102jyw5/california-quick-to-enforce-price-gouging-prohibitions-after-wildfires/)
- California enforced the law aggressively after the January 2025 LA wildfires. — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102jyw5/california-quick-to-enforce-price-gouging-prohibitions-after-wildfires/); [Gibson Dunn / Daily Journal](https://gibsondunn.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Smith-Rubin-Wildfire-housing-crisis-tests-California-price-gouging-law-Daily-Journal-1-30-25.pdf)

### Inferences
- Build an **emergency mode**: when a state of emergency covers a county served (e.g., San Mateo or Santa Clara; Bay Area declarations are common for storms, fires, and earthquakes), freeze recommended prices at the pre-declaration baseline, allow at most a +10% adjustment, show pros a warning, and keep a dated price history per pro and job type as baseline evidence. Turn off any "surge"/demand multipliers.
- Recommended prices should be advisory and transparent (methodology disclosed, e.g., "based on X jobs in your area"). This avoids deception claims, and keeping pros free to set prices helps on AB5.
- **Antitrust caution (inference; lawyer needed):** a platform that recommends uniform prices to competing independent businesses, especially by aggregating their non-public price data, risks being treated as a price-coordination hub. Recent algorithmic-pricing litigation (e.g., RealPage) and new California legislation on common pricing algorithms (2025, AB 325 amending the Cartwright Act — **not verified in this session**) are relevant. Use public or aggregated, non-binding ranges, and do not pressure pros to adopt them.
- California's honest-pricing law SB 478 (eff. July 1, 2024; background knowledge, unverified here) requires advertised prices to include all mandatory fees. Recommended or displayed prices should not hide mandatory service or trip fees.

### Gaps
- Status and text of AB 325 (Cartwright Act amendment on common pricing algorithms) were not verified.
- No source specifically addresses platforms that recommend prices during emergencies under § 396 (versus the seller).

## 7. Payments: no-payments model vs. Stripe invoicing (money transmission)

### Takeaway
Without payment processing, NextTrust collects only its own subscription fees, which is not money transmission. If NextTrust later collects client payments for pros, California's Money Transmission Act could apply unless the **agent-of-payee exemption** (Fin. Code § 2010(l), plus 2021 DFPI regulations) fits. Using Stripe Connect, where Stripe is the licensed processor and funds flow to pros' connected accounts, is the standard way marketplaces avoid holding funds. Structure and ToS still matter.

### Cited Findings
- Fin. Code § 2010(l) exempts a transaction in which the recipient is an **agent of the payee** under a preexisting written contract, payment to the agent satisfies the payer's obligation to the payee, and other conditions are met. It was added by AB 2209 (2014). — [DFPI agent-of-payee](https://dbo.ca.gov/2021/02/25/agent-of-payee-exemption/); [Cooley 2021](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2021/2021-06-28-california-rulemaking-agent-of-a-payee-exemption-money-transmission-licensing)
- The 2021 DFPI regulations require that the payment be for goods or services other than money transmission. The exemption does not cover agents of the payor/sender or open-loop stored value. — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2021/2021-06-28-california-rulemaking-agent-of-a-payee-exemption-money-transmission-licensing); [MoFo](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/200921-california-agent-payee-exemption-shrinking)
- DFPI has issued fact-specific opinion letters applying the exemption to online marketplaces, including an "online marketplace for independent contractors". Older DBO guidance took a stricter view of marketplaces that receive buyer funds to transmit to sellers. — [DFPI opinion letters: marketplace for independent contractors](https://dfpi.ca.gov/rules-enforcement/laws-and-regulations/opinion-letters-by-law-subject/agent-of-payee-exemption-for-online-travel-marketplace-payroll-processing-exemption-agent-of-payee-exemption-for-online-marketplace-for-independent-contractors-internet-escrow-agent-not-engaging-in/); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/publications/2019/03/californiaclarifymoneytransmissionactexemptionthroughrulemaking.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Phase 1 (subscriptions only):** no money-transmission issue. Subscription billing is just NextTrust selling its own service. Key issues are instead California's automatic renewal law (clear terms, affirmative consent, easy online cancellation; background knowledge, unverified here) and SB 478 fee transparency.
- **Phase 2 (Stripe invoicing):** the safest pattern is for each pro to invoice the client directly through Stripe (Connect "direct charges" or Standard/Express accounts, with the pro as merchant of record), so funds go to the pro's Stripe account and NextTrust never holds them. If NextTrust becomes merchant of record or holds funds (destination charges, escrow, holding funds until job completion), rely on the agent-of-payee exemption by contract: written appointment of NextTrust as the pro's agent, with payment to NextTrust discharging the client's debt. Get fintech counsel.
- **Licensing risk increases with payments:** if NextTrust bills clients for construction work or sets job prices, it looks more like a contractor (see §1 Gaps). Invoices must show the pro's name and license number, and CSLB home-improvement contract rules (B&P § 7159: down-payment limits, written contract contents; background knowledge, unverified) apply to the pro's contract. The platform's invoice templates must not break them.
- Stripe's role (it is a licensed money transmitter and handles KYC on connected accounts) is widely relied on, but no source was retrieved confirming exactly how Stripe Connect structures map to § 2010(l).

### Gaps
- No primary source on Stripe Connect and California MTA treatment. Check Stripe's Connect documentation on "merchant of record / charge types" and Stripe's licensing pages, and get fintech counsel.
- B&P § 7159 down-payment cap (10% or $1,000, whichever is less, for home-improvement contracts, per background knowledge) is unverified here and relevant if NextTrust ever collects deposits.
- Local business registration (Redwood City business license tax, San Mateo County) for NextTrust itself was not researched.
