# Pro Software & Lead-Gen Spend, Flat-Fee Pro Marketplaces, Payments, and Cold Start (for NextTrust Marketplace, Bay Area)

Research note, compiled 2026-11-15. Method limitation: in this session WebFetch could not reach vendor sites (getjobber.com, housecallpro.com and stripe.com all failed DNS because of the sandbox network allowlist). Every figure below comes from WebSearch result summaries of the cited pages. Vendor pricing pages could not be opened directly, so most prices come from third-party aggregators that disagree with each other. Treat them as ranges and re-check them on the official pages before publishing anything.

## 1. Field-service software pricing (Housecall Pro, Jobber, ServiceTitan, Workiz, FieldEdge, Kickserv), per-user/per-tech models, most-used features, integrations

### Takeaway
SMB field-service software in 2026 costs roughly $30–$80/mo for a solo operator, $150–$330/mo for teams of about 5–8, and $245–$500+ per technician per month for ServiceTitan at the enterprise end. Vendors price in two ways: tiered bundles with a set number of included users (Housecall Pro, Workiz, Kickserv) or a per-user or per-technician charge (Jobber's extra users, ServiceTitan, FieldEdge). That gives a direct anchor for NextTrust's per-technician price: small shops already pay about $25–$60 per user/month for tooling.

### Cited Findings
- **Jobber**: the Core entry plan is cited at $29/mo billed annually for 1 user; other sources say $39/mo, or $49 billed monthly. Extra users are reported at about $29/mo each. The AI Receptionist add-on is $99/mo. — [Jobber academy / aggregator summary](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/housecall-pro-competitors/); [RivetOps comparison](https://www.rivetops.io/servicetitan-vs-jobber-vs-housecall-pro); [ToTheMax Media](https://tothemaxmedia.com/blog/jobber-vs-servicetitan-vs-housecall-pro)
- **Housecall Pro**: Basic is $59/mo annual or $79 month-to-month for 1 user. Essentials is about $149 annual ($189 monthly) with 5 users. MAX is about $299 annual ($329 monthly) with 8 users. One source says the Individual/Teams toggle changes this, e.g. Essentials at $229 for 1 user vs $299 for 5. — [ContractorToolStack HCP pricing](https://contractortoolstack.com/software/housecall-pro/pricing/); [Opsler](https://opsler.com/compare/housecall-pro/)
- Housecall Pro's official site advertises a **14-day free trial of the top (MAX) plan, no credit card needed**, with a reminder before the trial ends. — [Housecall Pro features](https://housecallpro.com/features)
- Housecall Pro card processing is advertised as **"starting at 2.59%"** on its official site. Third parties put both HCP and Jobber at about 2.9% + $0.30. — [Housecall Pro pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/); [ContractorApps Jobber vs HCP](https://contractorapps.pages.dev/comparisons/jobber-vs-housecall-pro)
- **ServiceTitan** publishes no prices. servicetitan.com/pricing lists Starter, Essentials and The Works with "Request Pricing" and describes the model as **per technician**. Third-party estimates range from **$245–$500+ per technician/month** to $500–$1,200 per user/month, plus onboarding fees. — [RivetOps](https://www.rivetops.io/servicetitan-vs-jobber-vs-housecall-pro); [Dinoquote](https://www.dinoquote.com/blog/servicetitan-vs-housecall-pro-vs-jobber/)
- **Workiz**: the last published rates were Kickstart **$225 (3 users)**, Standard **$275 (5 users, +$46/extra user)** and Pro **$325 (5 users, +$54/extra user)**, billed annually. A June 2026 archive capture still showed them, but the live page has since gone quote-only. Another source says it starts at $65/mo, with plumbing/HVAC shops typically paying $169–$299/mo. — [Fieldproxy FSM pricing database](https://www.fieldproxy.ai/fsm-software-pricing); [StackScored HCP vs Workiz](https://www.stackscored.com/pricing/field-service-management/compare/housecall-pro-vs-workiz/); [Skillmammoth](https://skillmammoth.com/blog/housecall-pro-alternatives)
- **FieldEdge** is quote-only. User reports put it at about **$100 per office user + $125 per technician per month**, month to month. — [ServiceAgent FieldEdge pricing](https://serviceagent.ai/blogs/fieldedge-pricing/); [Rework FieldEdge alternatives](https://resources.rework.com/tools/field-service/best-fieldedge-alternatives)
- **Kickserv** has the most transparent pricing, but sources disagree on the entry price ($47/mo vs $60/mo). One source mentions a free tier. Plans are tiered by user count. — [Fieldproxy Kickserv](https://www.fieldproxy.ai/fsm-software-pricing/kickserv); [Costbench](https://www.costbench.com/software/field-service-management/)
- **Features pros buy for**: roundups describe Housecall Pro as built around an **online customer booking page, automated reminders, post-job review requests and a drag-and-drop dispatch board**. Jobber is "the most popular field service tool for small contractors", covering scheduling, quoting and invoicing. Must-haves are listed as drag-and-drop calendar, automated reminders, crew assignment, mobile app and invoicing integration, with online booking often listed as "nice-to-have". — [SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/hub/scheduling/best-scheduling-software-for-contractors); [BuildOutreach toolkit](https://toolkit.buildoutreach.io/blog/best-scheduling-software-contractors.html)
- **Integrations**: Capterra reviewers confirm Housecall Pro connects to QuickBooks Online, Google Calendar, Zapier, CallRail, Gusto and Meta for Business. Google Calendar is among Jobber's most-cited integrations, but one review says Jobber's Google Calendar sync is **not real-time (about every 24 h)**. HCP's booking widget can be embedded on a website or linked from a Google Business Profile. — [Software Advice Jobber profile](https://www.softwareadvice.com/field-service-management/jobber-profile/); [ContractorApps](https://contractorapps.pages.dev/comparisons/jobber-vs-housecall-pro)

### Inferences
- The willingness-to-pay anchor for a 1–10 technician shop is about $50–$330/mo for full FSM software. NextTrust's subscription will be compared against this line item and against lead spend (section 2).
- Pricing by number of technicians is the industry norm (ServiceTitan, FieldEdge, Workiz extra seats, HCP team tiers), so pros will see per-tech pricing as familiar and fair. Bundles of "N included techs" (e.g. 1 / up to 5 / up to 10) are more common below 10 users than pure per-seat pricing.
- Since most pros already use Jobber or HCP, NextTrust should integrate (Google Calendar two-way sync, booking link, possibly a Jobber/HCP API or Zapier) rather than replace FSM software. Real-time Google Calendar sync is a weakness in at least one incumbent (Jobber), so it is a cheap differentiator.
- A "14-day free trial of the top tier, no card" is the category's standard onboarding tactic.

### Gaps
- Official 2026 pricing pages could not be fetched (sandbox DNS block). All figures are from aggregators and conflict, e.g. Jobber $29 vs $39 entry.
- No quantitative survey of feature usage shares was found (e.g. "% of pros using online booking"). ServiceTitan/Housecall Pro industry reports with such data did not surface.

## 2. What pros spend on marketing/leads per month (Thumbtack, Angi, Google LSA, Yelp); cost per acquired plumbing customer

### Takeaway
Plumbing leads cost about $25–$90 each on shared marketplaces and Google LSA. Cost per booked job is much higher: about $170 for LSA, $250 for Thumbtack, and $540 to over $1,000 for Angi, because shared leads go to several pros and many never respond. Home-service firms typically spend about 5–12% of revenue on marketing. All of this comes from vendor and agency blogs, not independent surveys.

### Cited Findings
- Shared marketplace leads (Angi, Thumbtack) cost about **$25–$80** for plumbing. LSA leads cost about **$35–$90**. — [ServicePro plumbing leads](https://servicepro.co/blog/plumbing-leads)
- Angi plumbing leads are cited at **$65–$150** in another roundup. Shared platforms come to **$600–$1,200 per booked job**. — [PipelineOn platforms 2026](https://pipelineon.com/blog/best-lead-generation-platforms-plumbing-contractors-2026/)
- One benchmark puts cost per booked job at **LSA about $168, Thumbtack about $250, Angi about $542**. Thumbtack leads have an estimated **~75% no-response rate**. — [PipelineOn plumbing leads](https://pipelineon.com/blog/plumbing-leads/)
- A SearchLight-based roundup reports an average of **about $57 per LSA lead** with an **~44% book rate**. — [Hero: plumbing lead costs](https://gowithhero.com/blog/how-much-do-plumbing-leads-cost)
- LSA leads go to one pro only, while Angi and Thumbtack send the same lead to several contractors. — [LaunchAdvisor Angi vs Thumbtack vs LSA](https://www.launchadvisor.co/guides/angi-thumbtack-google-lsa-comparison-plumbing-hvac-contractor)
- Angi is typically **$15–$85 per lead** (over $100 in high-value trades), plus an **annual fee commonly ~$300/yr**. Angi does not publish pro pricing. — [SimplyWise lead-gen companies](https://www.simplywise.com/blog/home-service-lead-generation-companies/); [FitSmallBusiness on Angi](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/what-is-angis-list/)
- Yelp ads for plumbers are cost-per-click, about **$10–$40/click**, with typical minimum monthly spend of **$150–$300**. Pros complain about "$80 Yelp leads that never call back". — [Yelp for plumbers review](https://icanpayyouonmonday-com.pages.dev/blog/yelp-leads-plumbers); [GetNextPhone Yelp for contractors](https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/yelp-advertising-for-contractors)
- Thumbtack uses a credits/pay-per-contact model. In competitive markets most plumbers reportedly need "Promote" to get volume. — [Thumbtack for plumbers review](https://icanpayyouonmonday-com.pages.dev/blog/thumbtack-for-plumbers)
- Nextdoor is described as an underused channel built on a verified business profile and neighbor recommendations, "often producing some of the lowest cost-per-booked-job numbers in plumbing" (promotional source). There is no fixed published ad price, and "Local Deals" is budget-controlled. — [AdWave best advertising for plumbers](https://adwave.com/resources/best-advertising-for-plumbers); [SimplyWise](https://www.simplywise.com/blog/home-service-lead-generation-companies/)
- Marketing budget benchmarks: healthy home-service businesses spend **6–12% of revenue**. Other guides give a 5–15% working range, and 7–10% for HVAC. The SBA's guideline is 7–8% for firms under $5M revenue. Gartner 2025 CMO survey: 7.7%, enterprise-skewed. — [HSM Pro budget benchmarks](https://hsmpro.com/resources/home-services-marketing-budget-benchmarks); [Slice of the Pie](https://sliceofthepie.co/blog/home-service-marketing-budget.html); [PipelineOn marketing spend](https://pipelineon.com/blog/marketing-spend/)
- Scorpion published a "2026 State of Home Services Marketing Report" (about 1,000 home-service leaders surveyed, Feb 2026). Its spend figure was not retrieved. — [Scorpion (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorpion_(company))

### Inferences
- If a small plumbing shop gets even 3–5 booked jobs/month from NextTrust, the equivalent value at LSA/Thumbtack rates is about $500–$1,250/mo, and $1,600+ at Angi rates. A flat fee of a few hundred dollars per month is easy to justify **if** NextTrust can show booked jobs. The pitch should be framed in cost per booked job, not cost per lead.
- Pros' biggest pain points are shared leads and paying for leads that never respond. "No lead fees, exclusive requests, flat price" goes straight at the most-cited complaint about Angi, Thumbtack and Yelp.
- At 6–12% of revenue, a 3-tech plumbing shop doing about $750k/yr spends roughly $45k–$90k/yr ($3.75k–$7.5k/mo) on marketing. A NextTrust subscription would be a small slice of that.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) 2025–2026 survey of monthly lead spend per plumber was found, and no Bay Area-specific CPL. Bay Area CPLs are likely above national averages, but no source was found to confirm this.
- No official Thumbtack, Angi or Nextdoor pro price lists.

## 3. Flat-fee / subscription marketplaces for pros (Checkatrade, Angi history, Nextdoor, Houzz Pro, Porch, BuildZoom): pricing, outcomes, trial/onboarding tactics

### Takeaway
The clearest flat-membership precedent is UK Checkatrade: entry membership from £30 + VAT/mo, no joining fee, with paid tiers and add-ons that take typical all-in spend to about £100–£300/mo. US platforms mostly charge per lead (Angi, Thumbtack, Porch) or sell software subscriptions (Houzz Pro, $99–$399+/mo). Angie's List abandoned its paid *consumer* membership in 2016 and moved to free consumer access with the pro side paying.

### Cited Findings
- **Checkatrade**: the basic plan starts **from £30 + VAT per month**, with **no joining fee** (Checkatrade's own blog, updated Jan 2025). — [Checkatrade: Does Checkatrade cost money?](https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/trade/start-business/does-checkatrade-cost-money/)
- Checkatrade's higher tiers (Fixed/Growth) are priced per trade and per area after a sales call ("from £59/mo" per a third-party guide). Sponsored Listings are "from £100/mo". Member-reported per-lead fees are about £5–£40. Typical all-in spend is **£100–£300/mo**, per third-party and member reports. Checkatrade has also introduced "flexible membership". — [Whito: Checkatrade cost](https://whito.co.uk/trades/checkatrade-cost-uk/); [Checkatrade flexible membership blog](https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/?p=22431); [Checkatrade membership ROI](https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/trade/grow-business/checkatrade-membership-roi/); [Trade2Base: is Checkatrade worth it 2026](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/is-checkatrade-worth-it-2026)
- **Angie's List history**: in July 2016 it made its basic consumer membership free (previously about $9.99/yr). Silver ($24.99/yr) and Gold ($99.99/yr) tiers remained. The company shifted toward pros paying for advertising and leads, and HomeAdvisor Pro became "Angi Leads". — [Fortune 2016](https://fortune.com/2016/03/03/angies-list-subscription/); [CNBC 2016](https://www.cnbc.com/2016/07/13/need-a-plumber-angies-list-reviews-are-now-free.html); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/angies-list-now-offering-free-membership-nationwide-300297827.html); [Angi Inc. (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angi_Inc.)
- **Angi Key** ($29.99/yr, 20% off services booked on Angi) is a *homeowner* membership, not a pro one. — [SimplyWise](https://www.simplywise.com/blog/home-service-lead-generation-companies/)
- **Houzz Pro**: a free tier plus Design $99, Pro $199 and Teams from $399/mo (per SimplyWise, citing Houzz's pricing page). Capterra lists from $399/mo and G2 lists a $249 Pro edition. Advertising is an add-on from about $499/mo. — [Capterra Houzz Pro pricing](https://www.capterra.com/p/199689/Houzz-Pro/pricing/); [G2 Houzz Pro pricing](https://www.g2.com/products/houzz-pro/pricing); [SimplyWise](https://www.simplywise.com/blog/home-service-lead-generation-companies/)
- **Porch**: no published price. One contractor guide cites a "Vetted Network" membership at **$360/yr**, with pay-per-lead pricing and optional monthly subscriptions. — [SimplyWise](https://www.simplywise.com/blog/home-service-lead-generation-companies/)
- **Nextdoor**: no fixed pro price, and Local Deals are budget-controlled. — [SimplyWise](https://www.simplywise.com/blog/home-service-lead-generation-companies/)
- **Trial tactic benchmark**: Housecall Pro gives a 14-day full-access trial of its top plan with no card. Jobber is also cited with a 14-day trial. — [Housecall Pro features](https://housecallpro.com/features); [ContractorApps](https://contractorapps.pages.dev/comparisons/jobber-vs-housecall-pro)

### Inferences
- Checkatrade's model ("vetted badge + directory listing" at a low flat monthly fee, with upsells for visibility and area) is the closest analog to NextTrust's concept. Over time, though, Checkatrade layered on per-lead and sponsored fees, which suggests pure flat pricing gets pressure to add visibility-based upsells. NextTrust should decide early whether to sell ranking or keep a trust-based neutral ranking as its differentiator.
- Angi's 2016 move shows that consumer-side fees kill demand. Keep the homeowner side free and charge pros.
- Price-point corridor for a flat pro membership: about $30–$60/mo entry (Checkatrade, Porch at $30/mo equivalent) up to $199–$399/mo for software-plus-visibility (Houzz Pro).

### Gaps
- **BuildZoom**: no pricing or model details surfaced in searches. Not covered.
- No quantitative outcome data (churn, retention, ROI) for Checkatrade, Houzz Pro or Porch memberships beyond Checkatrade's own marketing blog.
- No primary source on Angi's pro-side annual fee history (the ~$300/yr figure is third-party).

## 4. Invoicing/payments: Stripe Invoicing and Stripe Connect fees; invoicing without holding funds; tipping and deposits

### Takeaway
With Stripe Connect **direct charges**, the pro's own connected account is the merchant of record and receives funds directly. The platform can take an optional application fee, or none, without ever holding funds. Stripe Invoicing adds about 0.4% per paid invoice on top of card and ACH fees. Connect costs $0 for Standard accounts and $2 per active account/month plus 0.25% + $0.25 per payout for Express/Custom accounts on platform-managed pricing.

### Cited Findings
- **Stripe Invoicing Starter is 0.4% per paid invoice**, charged only when the invoice is paid through Stripe. Unpaid, voided, uncollectible and paid-outside-Stripe invoices are not charged. Plus is cited at 0.5% by third parties. A $2 per-invoice cap is unconfirmed. — [Stripe support: Invoicing pricing](https://support.stripe.com/questions/stripe-invoicing-pricing); [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/stripe-invoicing-fees-explained); [Flexprice](https://flexprice.io/blog/stripe-pricing-breakdown-2026)
- **Stripe Connect**: Standard accounts carry no platform fee, since Stripe bills the seller directly. Express/Custom accounts cost **$2 per active account/month + 0.25% + $0.25 per payout**. Large platforms can get custom pricing. — [Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing); [NerdWallet Stripe fees](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/software/learn/stripe-fees)
- **Direct charges make the connected account the merchant of record.** The charge lives on the connected account, and the platform can collect an `application_fee_amount`, which goes to the platform's own Stripe account. Stripe charges no extra fee on the application fee itself, which must be positive and below the charge. Application fees are not refunded automatically on refunds. — [Stripe docs: direct charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges?lang=php); [Stripe docs: create direct charges](https://stripe.com/docs/connect/direct-charges)
- Under destination/separate charges the platform is the default merchant of record, unless `on_behalf_of` is set to the connected account. — [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges?lang=php); [Stripe Sessions 2026: multiparty payment flows](https://stripe.com/sessions/2026/splitting-the-check-architecting)
- Housecall Pro advertises card processing from 2.59%. The third-party norm for FSM apps is about 2.9% + $0.30. — [Housecall Pro pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/); [ContractorApps](https://contractorapps.pages.dev/comparisons/jobber-vs-housecall-pro)

### Inferences
- For a "no transaction fee" brand, the simplest setup is **Stripe Connect Standard accounts + direct charges with no application fee**. Each pro has their own Stripe account, pays Stripe's standard rates (about 2.9% + 30¢ for cards per Stripe's public pricing, not verified here) and gets paid directly. NextTrust holds no funds, avoids money-transmitter and payout-liability issues, and pays $0 Connect platform fees. The cost is less control over branding and disputes.
- **Deposits**: can be done as a separate upfront payment or invoice on the pro's account (e.g. Invoicing or Payment Links for X% before the visit), then a final invoice that credits the deposit. **Tips**: can be added as an optional line item or custom amount on the final payment page. Both flow straight to the pro under direct charges.
- If NextTrust wants revenue from payments later, a small application fee on direct charges is possible. That would weaken the "no fees" message, so it is probably better to keep payments revenue at zero and monetize only through the subscription.

### Gaps
- Stripe's official pages could not be fetched (DNS block). Current US card rates, the Invoicing cap and the exact 2026 Connect fee schedule need verification on stripe.com.
- No Stripe documentation was retrieved on native tipping or deposit features within Invoicing/Connect. The approaches above are inferences.

## 5. Cold-start strategies for local service marketplaces (supply first, density, concierge MVP); case studies

### Takeaway
Most marketplaces start supply-constrained: 14 of 17, about 80%, in Lenny's survey started with supply. The winning playbook for local services is to recruit a small, dense pool of pros (about 15–30 per city or category to start), give them value that doesn't depend on demand yet (tools, a trust profile, or guaranteed jobs), and keep to one geography and one category until that "atomic network" sustains itself.

### Cited Findings
- **14 of 17 marketplaces (~80%) Lenny surveyed started with supply.** The median number of levers big marketplaces used to kickstart supply was **two**. — [Lenny's: How to kickstart and scale a marketplace](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace); [Lenny on X](https://x.com/lennysan/status/1167088120132562945)
- The most popular early supply channel for labor marketplaces is posting generic roles on job boards (Indeed, ZipRecruiter, Craigslist). Thumbtack co-founder Sander Daniels: "We used Indeed.com and simplyhired.com." About a third of the marketplaces studied piggybacked on Craigslist. — [Lenny's: Kickstarting supply in a labor marketplace](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/labor-marketplace-supply-growth); [Lenny's: kickstart (part)](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace-9ee)
- **Thumbtack** went broad by category instead of constraining, and seeded supply first ("all that matters is supply"). Its lesson, via NFX discussion with its CEO: a cold-start solution must deliver **standalone value**, because supply won't stay for promised future demand. Thumbtack monetizes at the point of contact. — [The Marketplace Guide: Thumbtack](https://themarketplaceguide.com/articles/how-thumbtack-bootstrapped-a-17b-marketplace-the-network-independent-value-playbook/); [Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/labor-marketplace-supply-growth)
- **TaskRabbit**: an executive says "I don't think we were ever supply constrained". There were thousands of applicants on the waitlist, a Tasker application fee was later charged to reduce volume, and demand was the harder side. Another (secondary) source says early Taskers were recruited manually via Craigslist and local Boston groups. Both can be true at different stages. — [Lenny's: kickstart](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace-9ee); [Sharetribe: build like TaskRabbit](https://www.sharetribe.com/create/how-to-build-website-like-taskrabbit/)
- **Urban Company (UrbanClap)** was founded Nov 2014 in Delhi with founder roles split across demand, supply and tech. It began as a **lead-gen marketplace where pros paid for introductions**. Supply was the hardest part: beauticians had to be recruited **face to face**, with a **minimum guarantee of 2–3 jobs/day and a legal contract**. It ran early training centers and vetted pros for skills, behavior and hygiene. It tested in Delhi for 4 months and was in 8 cities by end of 2016. It later pivoted to a full-stack model for quality control. — [YourStory: UrbanClap product roadmap](https://yourstory.com/2021/03/product-roadmap-urban-company-home-services-startup-survived-test-time); [Outlook Business: "90% of what we did didn't work"](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/lead-story/90-of-what-we-did-didnt-work-6347); [Brand Hopper](https://thebrandhopper.com/featured-startups/urban-company-story-history-business-model-funding-growth/); [Seed to Scale: first 500 days](https://www.seedtoscale.com/content/untold-seed-stories-first-500-days-of-urban-company)
- **Andrew Chen (a16z), *The Cold Start Problem***: build the smallest stable, self-sustaining "atomic network" first. It is often smaller than founders expect (Tinder: about 200–500 people per campus), and density beats total size. Solve the "hard side" (usually sellers) first, then replicate network by network. — [Andrew Clark summary](https://andrewclark.co.uk/product-book-summaries/the-cold-start-problem); [Medium summary](https://medium.com/twosapp/the-cold-start-problem-how-to-start-and-scale-network-effects-by-andrew-chen-813f0668c70f)
- For local services, as few as **15–30 active providers in a single city** may be enough to start. Thumbtack and TaskRabbit both concentrated on specific cities and categories before expanding. "Supply first" is not universal and depends on which side is scarcer. — [The Marketplace Guide: cold start pattern](https://themarketplaceguide.com/patterns/cold-start/)

### Inferences
- For NextTrust (Bay Area, plumbing/electrical): start with **one sub-region** (e.g. one county or a cluster of cities) and **2 trades**. Hand-recruit about 20–30 vetted pros, then concentrate demand-gen (Nextdoor, local SEO, community groups) in that same area, so each pro sees requests within weeks.
- The Thumbtack and Urban Company lessons point to *standalone value* for pros while demand is thin: free or extended trials, a verified trust profile/badge they can use on their own site and GBP, a booking link, Google Calendar sync and invoicing. That way the subscription is worth keeping before demand arrives. A "founding member" period that is free until the pro receives N requests, or a money-back/credit guarantee, mirrors Urban Company's job guarantee at lower cost.
- A concierge MVP fits: manually match homeowner requests to pros by phone or SMS before building automation, and use that to measure booked-jobs-per-pro, the metric that justifies the flat fee (see section 2).
- Charging subscription from day one runs against the "standalone value" lesson. Consider delaying billing until density exists, or price the early tier low (Checkatrade-like £30 ≈ $40/mo).

### Gaps
- No NFX article text was retrieved directly (only the secondhand Thumbtack-CEO citation). No a16z marketplace-specific posts on home services beyond Chen's book summaries.
- No primary-source details of TaskRabbit's Boston launch tactics. No explicit "concierge MVP" case study for home-services marketplaces was found.
